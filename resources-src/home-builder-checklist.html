Home-builder checklist

What to check at each stage of building a villa or independent house in India, from buying the plot to moving in. Tick each item as you go.

1. Before you design

  • Title deed, encumbrance certificate and mutation records checked by a lawyerConfirm the seller's ownership and that there are no loans or disputes on the land.
  • Land use and building rules confirmed with the local authorityZoning, permitted floor area (FSI/FAR), setbacks, height limit and parking rules for your plot.
  • Soil investigation report from a qualified geotechnical firmThe structural engineer needs it to design the foundations.
  • A total budget written down, including a contingency of 10% or moreInclude design fees, approvals, utilities, interiors and furniture, not just construction.
  • A rough timeline agreed in your family, with the date you need to move in

2. Design

  • Written brief for the architect: rooms, sizes, how you live, budget and must-haves
  • Architect and structural engineer appointed with written scopes and fees
  • MEP (plumbing, electrical, air-conditioning) designed, not left to the contractor
  • Full drawing set before tender: plans, sections, elevations, structural, MEP, door and window schedule
  • Specifications written down: brands and grades of cement, steel, tiles, fittings, wiring
  • Design reviewed against the budget before drawings are frozenChanging a drawing on paper is cheap; changing it on site is not.

3. Approvals

  • Building plan sanction received before any work starts on site
  • Other approvals your project needs identified early (for example tree cutting, fire NOC for taller buildings, environmental clearance for large sites)
  • Temporary electricity and water connections arranged for construction
  • Copies of every approval kept in one file, with validity dates noted

4. Choosing the contractor

  • At least three contractors invited, all pricing the same drawings and bill of quantities
  • Past projects visited and previous clients called
  • Bids compared line by line, including what each one leaves out
  • Written contract signed: scope, price, programme, payment stages, penalties for delay, retention and defects periodRetention of around 5% of each bill, released after the defects period, is common practice.
  • Payments linked to completed work, not to dates
  • Contractor's all-risk insurance and labour registrations confirmed

5. During construction: inspect before it gets covered

  • Setting out checked against the drawings before excavation
  • Foundation depth and soil checked by the structural engineer before concreting
  • Reinforcement checked (bar sizes, spacing, cover, laps) before every concrete pour
  • Concrete cube samples taken and tested at 7 and 28 days
  • Shuttering and props checked before slab pours; props left for the specified time
  • Electrical conduits and plumbing lines checked and pressure-tested before plastering
  • Waterproofing of terraces, bathrooms and tanks tested by ponding before tiling
  • Materials delivered match the agreed brands and grades; delivery notes kept
  • Weekly site photos and a short progress note kept, with every change agreed in writing
  • Bills checked against measured work before you pay

6. Finishing and handover

  • Snag list walked room by room; every item fixed and re-checked
  • All services tested: water pressure, drainage, every switch and socket, AC, pumps
  • As-built drawings, manuals and warranties handed over in one file
  • Occupancy or completion certificate obtained
  • Final account agreed and retention release date written down
Signs you need help: bills arriving faster than work, drawings changed on site without paper, the contractor asking for advances to keep going, or no one able to tell you the finish date.
